01

The Knot Worldwide · Paper

The engineering behind Paper's growth

Commerce platform · Payments · Experimentation · Team building

Challenge

Paper had to move off Drupal and grow online in a category considered to be in decline. The focus: The Knot and WeddingWire in the US, in English.

What I led

  • Built the team from scratch: hiring profiles, and a budget for people, tools and infrastructure estimated with leadership and managed by me.
  • Drupal to Next.js and TypeScript, as the first multi-tenant application shared by The Knot and WeddingWire.
  • Checkout rescue: the project reached my team in August 2022, 18 months late and with no checkout or ecommerce tracking. We built both in three months and delivered in December, on date. Payments on Stripe and, since 2026, Klarna, which the team itself spotted and shipped ahead of Black Friday.
  • 8–10 experiments a year across the whole funnel, self-service banners and copy for marketing, and an automated, rule-based Google Ads feed.
  • European pilot on Bodas.net, Mariages.net and Matrimonio.com: outsourced, but under my responsibility (incidents and vendor) until it closed in May 2025, when we removed the European verticals' code.

Impact

  • Year-over-year revenue growth in a category considered to be in decline.
  • The checkout is still in production today; all of Paper's online revenue goes through it.
  • Three people on the team grew into leads.

02

Trovit · Classifieds search

Making the systems billing depended on predictable

Billing-critical tracking · Kafka · Email delivery · Search APIs

Challenge

Tracking was the basis of the billing model. Events left PHP without strict types, every Kafka message was typed by hand and, by company policy, the Java side did not validate. Errors reached the dataset and had to be recomputed by hand with ad hoc scripts.

What I led

  • Rebuilt the tracking API and moved validation upstream: a Java integration suite in the deploy pipeline that started the Java consumer and simulated every message (PHP, Kafka, Kafka Streams in Java and Scala, data lake) before touching production.
  • Rebuilt the company-wide email service, around 100,000 emails a day: legacy code nobody could touch, IPs blacklisted as spam and no SLA. I defined the SLAs for delivery speed and machine and IP usage.
  • The search API for every vertical in 57 countries, around 30,000 requests a minute. I set a sub-100 ms target: in-memory endpoints at about 1 ms, caching for the rest, and Grafana by endpoint and country.
  • Decoupled the frontends from the legacy search engine, moving PHP towards Next.js, with quality and SEO tests where there were none.

Impact

  • Manual recomputations disappeared completely, with no further billing-data failures.
  • No IP was blacklisted again, and emails always went out within SLA.
  • Every traffic peak explained by endpoint and country.

03

Adsmurai · Marketing technology

Spotting the need for a data team, creating it and building the data lake

Influence without authority · Data platform · Ad platforms

Challenge

Campaign data from Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat was scattered, three teams kept reimplementing the same ingestion, and the existing Data team, doing computer vision to label feeds, worked on its own.

What I led

  • Pushed for a Data Guild until it existed: four people, bringing in the existing Data team.
  • Built a data lake with Airflow and Snowflake that centralised campaign data from all four platforms. Snowflake was chosen by others; the pipelines were mine.
  • Decided to move the UGC project from Vert.x to Spring Boot for simplicity and better documentation, and introduced Elasticsearch.
  • All with a five-person team run like a startup.

Impact

  • Three teams stopped reimplementing ingestion and worked from one source.
  • The foundation of Ads Composer, which gives clients their metrics and finds the best segments to invest in and replicate.
  • Faster, more reliable delivery on data work.

How I lead

Engineering that delivers what the business needs

Three principles, each backed by something I actually did.

01 · OWNERSHIP

Teams own outcomes, end to end

I give teams the whole problem, and they take it further. Klarna wasn't on anyone's roadmap: my team spotted it, we decided together and shipped it ahead of Black Friday.

02 · GROWTH

Grow people into leaders

Clear bars and real responsibility. Three people on my team grew into leads; our QA joined on the condition of moving from manual to automated testing, and is now QA Lead.

03 · DECISIONS

Technical calls follow the business

Fix what costs the business, within the constraints you have. At Trovit the Java side couldn't validate by policy, so I moved validation into the deploy pipeline, and manual billing fixes disappeared.
